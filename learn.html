5 reasons your website isn't bringing in calls

Most business websites don't fail because they look bad. They fail because they make it hard for a ready-to-buy visitor to take the next step.

If people are visiting your site but your phone isn't ringing, the problem is almost always one of these five things. The good news: every one of them is fixable.

1. It's slow on a phone

Most of your visitors are on their phone, often on a spotty connection. If your site takes more than a few seconds to load, a lot of them leave before they ever see what you do. Oversized images and bloated page builders are the usual culprits.

2. Your phone number is hard to find

Someone ready to call shouldn't have to hunt. Your number should be visible at the top of every page and tappable on mobile, with a clear button that says exactly what happens next, like "Call now" or "Get a quote."

3. It doesn't say what you do in five seconds

Visitors decide almost instantly whether they're in the right place. Your headline should say what you do and who you do it for in plain language. "Welcome to our website" wastes the most valuable spot on the page.

4. Google doesn't know what you're about

If your pages don't clearly describe your services, Google can't match you with people searching for them. At minimum:

  • Give every page a clear, specific title and description
  • Set up and fully complete your Google Business Profile
  • Describe each service on the page in the words your customers actually use

5. There's no proof

People trust other people. Real reviews, photos of your actual work, and results you can point to do more to earn a call than any amount of clever copy.

A website has one job: turn the people who find you into people who contact you. Everything on the page should serve that.

How often should your business post on social media?

The honest answer is "as often as you can keep up, forever." Here's how to figure out what that means for you.

Business owners ask us this constantly, and the answer surprises most of them: consistency matters far more than volume. A page that posts twice a week, every week, will almost always beat a page that posts every day for a month and then disappears.

Start with a pace you can hold

Two posts a week is a solid floor. It's enough to stay visible and build familiarity without burning out. Once that's automatic, step up to three or more, and add video.

Make video part of the mix

Short-form video is where most reach comes from on today's platforms. It doesn't need to be polished. Behind-the-scenes clips, quick tips, and before-and-afters of your work consistently outperform stock-looking graphics.

Get more from every piece of content

You don't need a new idea for every post. One good video can become:

  • Several short clips for different platforms
  • A photo post with a pulled quote
  • A story, a caption, and a talking point for next week

Pay attention to what works

Every month, look at which posts got the most views, shares and messages, then make more of those. Growth comes from doing more of what your audience already responds to.

Showing up reliably is the strategy. Everything else is optimization.

Websites 3 min read

Template or custom: which website does your business need?

Both can look great. The right choice depends on what your site actually has to do.

Choose a template build if…

  • You need a professional site online quickly
  • Your site's main job is to explain what you do and get people to contact you
  • You want the most affordable way to look established

A well-built template, customized to your brand, is the right answer for many service businesses. It's fast, mobile-friendly, and gets the job done.

Choose a custom build if…

  • You need online ordering, checkout, or bulk orders
  • Customers need to book, schedule, or submit detailed requests
  • You want to manage your own product catalog or database
  • You want to stand out in a crowded market with a design no one else has

Custom builds are designed around how your business actually runs, so the site does real work for you instead of just sitting there.

Still not sure?

Ask yourself: "What should a visitor be able to do on my site?" If the answer is "learn about us and call," start with a template. If it involves buying, booking, or logging in, you'll want custom.
